Maps show how the land around Vajska has changed: the Danube with its side arms and marshes, the Bođani woods and the spot where Lake Provala later formed. A map of today's village with every place on this site is on the [Map]({{< relref "map" >}}) page.

## Topographic maps of the Yugoslav People's Army, 1970s

The most detailed maps from the second half of the 20th century are the topographic maps of the Yugoslav People's Army (JNA), drawn and printed by its Military Geographical Institute (*Vojnogeografski institut*) in Belgrade. For decades they were secret: the margins carry the military classification and the note *"Kopiranje i umnožavanje zabranjeno"* (copying and reproduction forbidden). Today scans of many sheets are collected on [topografskakarta.com](https://www.topografskakarta.com/).

**These maps are published with the permission of Haxhi Berisha, the owner of [topografskakarta.com](https://www.topografskakarta.com/).** We show only the part of each sheet around Vajska. If you hold the rights to these maps and object to their publication here, please [write to us]({{< relref "author" >}}) and we will remove them.

### 1:25,000, sheets "Bačka Palanka 1-1" and "1-2", 1975–1976

{{< oldmap src="/img/jna25-vajska.jpg" poster="/img/jna25-vajska-960.jpg" x="620" y="560" size="0.7 MB" link="https://www.topografskakarta.com/jugo/download/srb_25/bpalanka_1/h91.html" alt="JNA topographic map 1:25,000, sheets Bačka Palanka 1-1 and 1-2: Vajska, Lake Provala and Bođani" >}}Joined details of sheets 377-1-1 “Bačka Palanka 1-1” (Labudnjača; second edition, printed in 1975) and [377-1-2 “Bačka Palanka 1-2”](https://www.topografskakarta.com/jugo/download/srb_25/bpalanka_2/h92.html) (Bač; second edition, printed in 1976) of the JNA topographic map 1:25,000; the content of both was updated in 1973. Vajska lies on the edge between the two sheets. Scan: topografskakarta.com, published with the permission of Haxhi Berisha.{{< /oldmap >}}

What you can find on this map:

- **Vajska** with its regular grid of streets and, south-west of the village, a lake labelled **Provala**; further south, Negina bara.
- **Bođani Monastery** (marked "Bođani", 83 m) on the road to the **village of Bođani**.
- The Vajs canal west of the village, Prekoselska bara to the north, Alpar and Guvno to the east and the large **Bođani forest** (*Bođanska šuma*) to the south-east.

## Austro-Hungarian maps

These maps were made by the Military Geographical Institute in Vienna. They are in the public domain and published on Wikimedia Commons.

### Special map 1:75,000, sheet "Vukovar", 1914

{{< oldmap src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/Vukovar_BV042104285.jpg/3840px-Vukovar_BV042104285.jpg" poster="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/Vukovar_BV042104285.jpg/960px-Vukovar_BV042104285.jpg" x="2150" y="1250" size="5.3 MB" link="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vukovar_BV042104285.jpg" alt="Special map of Austria-Hungary 1:75,000, sheet Vukovar (Zone 24, Column XX), 1914" >}}Sheet “Vukovar” (Zone 24, Column XX) of the Special Map of the Austro-Hungarian Monarchy 1:75,000, 1914. Public domain; scan by the Leibniz Institute for East and Southeast European Studies (IOS), Regensburg, via GeoPortOst.{{< /oldmap >}}

What you can find around Vajska:

- The village under its Hungarian name **Vajszka** (85 m), with the fields of Korlat and Alpár.
- **Kloster Bogyán (Kaluđer)**, Bođani Monastery, and the village of **Bogyán**.
- Selska bara and Tanki Lug, and fishermen's houses (*Fischerhaus*) on the Danube.
- **No Lake Provala yet**: the villagers date it to 1924 ([provala.rs](https://provala.rs/en/)).

### General map of Central Europe 1:200,000, sheet "Mistrowitz", about 1910

{{< oldmap src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/44/Mistrowitz_-_37-45.jpg" poster="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Mistrowitz_-_37-45.jpg/960px-Mistrowitz_-_37-45.jpg" x="1080" y="560" size="4.7 MB" link="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mistrowitz_-_37-45.jpg" alt="General map of Central Europe, sheet Mistrowitz, about 1910: Vajska and Bođani" >}}Sheet 37°45° “Mistrowitz” of the General Map of Central Europe 1:200,000, about 1910. Public domain; scan published by the Department of Cartography of Eötvös Loránd University, Budapest (László Zentai).{{< /oldmap >}}

Here Vajska is **Vajszka** (85 m), Bođani is **Bogyán**, Deronje is **Dernye**, and the large island in the Danube near Vukovar is called **Živi otok** (*Lebendige Insel*, "the living island"). The same sheet shows Rakovac on Fruška Gora at its eastern edge.
