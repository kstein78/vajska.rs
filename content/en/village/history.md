Written sources on Vajska are scarce online, so we are building this page step by step with the help of residents. This is what is confirmed so far.

## Old names

In historical lists of settlements the village also appears as **Tothwayzka**. The name belongs to a group of Danube villages with *Tót* in their names, where the Bačka **Šokci** lived or still live ([ZKVH, Naselje Šokac](https://zkvh.org.rs/storage/app/uploads/public/62f/f33/037/62ff33037caf8000026131.pdf)). Today's official names are Vajska, Hungarian *Vajszka* and *Józsefháza*, German *Wajska* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajska)).

## A shrinking village

Between 1961 and 2011 the population fell from 4,355 to 2,834, by a third ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajska)). As in many villages of Bačka, young people move to the cities.

## The ferry to Borovo, 1991

After the fighting at Borovo Selo in Croatia, a Danube ferry between Vajska and Borovo started on 4 May 1991. It reportedly carried between 10,000 and 30,000 passengers, among them Croat refugees from Borovo ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajska)).
